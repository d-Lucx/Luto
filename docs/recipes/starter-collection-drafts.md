## Measurements, aliases, and shared notes

- For these test cards only, use a 5ml teaspoon, 15ml tablespoon, and 240ml cup. These are declared test conventions, not a global app default. Actual measuring utensils vary.
- Spoon and cup equivalents below are volume conversions. A produce count is approximate; weigh the prepared ingredient where a mass is supplied. Do not infer mass from a cup without ingredient-specific data.
- Cooking times do not scale with servings. Retest pan size and batches when doubling. All quantities below are for two portions.
- Candidate aliases to review: `talong / eggplant / aubergine`, `sayote / chayote`, `sibuyas / onion`, `bawang / garlic`, `kamatis / tomato`, `itlog / egg`, `manok / chicken`, `chickpea / garbanzo bean`, and `coriander leaves / cilantro`. Preserve variety and ingredient form; chicken breast and thigh are not interchangeable aliases.
- Candidate technique explainers: safe chopping, sautéing, simmering, reducing, roasting, turning with a spatula, and checking doneness. Reuse existing taxonomy entries where they fit; new entries need review.
- Check package allergen information for sauces, spice blends, pasta, and substitutes. Allergen notes below identify obvious ingredients, not a complete cross-contact assessment.

FoodSafety.gov specifies poultry at **74°C / 165°F**, egg dishes at **71°C / 160°F**, and whole eggs with firm whites and yolks. Use a food thermometer for chicken; browning or elapsed time does not establish safety. These drafts use fully cooked eggs rather than promising runny yolks. [Official cooking temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## R01 — Adobong Manok

**Status:** Untested. **Serves:** 2. **Equipment:** 24cm deep pan with lid, board, knife, spoon, food thermometer. **Contains:** soy; usually wheat in regular soy sauce.

| Ingredient | Test quantity | Household description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boneless, skinless chicken thigh | 500g | Cut into roughly 4cm pieces; weigh rather than count thighs |
| Onion, prepared | 100g | About 1 small onion, sliced |
| Garlic, prepared | 12g | About 4 cloves, chopped |
| Neutral oil | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Regular unsweetened soy sauce | 45ml | 3 tbsp |
| Cane or distilled white vinegar, approximately 5% acidity | 30ml | 2 tbsp; not concentrated cleaning vinegar |
| Water | 120ml | ½ test cup |
| Dried bay leaf | 1 leaf | Whole; remove before serving |
| Ground black pepper | ¼ tsp | Optional |

1. Prepare onion and garlic before handling chicken. Put the cut chicken in a separate bowl; clean hands and surfaces after raw-poultry contact.
2. Heat oil over medium heat. Stir onion for about 4 minutes, until becoming translucent. Add garlic for 30 seconds, keeping it pale.
3. Add chicken and turn for 4–5 minutes. Light browning develops flavor; the center is still uncooked.
4. Add soy sauce, vinegar, water, bay, and optional pepper. Once bubbling, lower the heat, cover, and simmer gently for about 15 minutes. Look for occasional small bubbles, not violent boiling.
5. Check the thickest chicken pieces reach 74°C / 165°F. Continue cooking if needed. Uncover and reduce for approximately 5–10 minutes, stirring, until sauce lightly coats the chicken. Add water by the tablespoon if it dries before the chicken is done.
6. Remove bay leaf. Taste sauce only after the chicken is cooked. Serve two portions; rice is a separate accompaniment.

**Optional explainer:** Reducing means letting water evaporate to concentrate a sauce. It also concentrates salt; do not automatically add more soy sauce.

**Test questions:** Is the sauce balanced with the named soy/vinegar brands? Is chicken tender when safe? Record final sauce volume. Gluten-free tamari is a candidate substitution requiring its own flavor and allergen check.

**Reference and changes:** Lalaine Manalo's [Chicken Adobo, Kawaling Pinoy](https://www.kawalingpinoy.com/chicken-adobo/) provides a Filipino braising reference. This draft uses boneless thigh, omits potatoes and marination, and uses a different liquid ratio and sequence. It is not that publisher's tested recipe.

## R02 — Tortang Talong

**Status:** Untested. **Serves:** 2. **Equipment:** oven, tray, fork, shallow bowl, 24cm frying pan, broad spatula, thermometer. **Contains:** egg.

| Ingredient | Test quantity | Household description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slender Asian eggplants | 2, about 300–400g total before roasting | Keep stems; record actual mass |
| Large eggs | 2 | Beat together |
| Neutral oil | 30ml | 2 tbsp total: half for each batch |
| Fine salt | ¼ tsp | For the egg mixture |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | Optional |

1. Heat a conventional oven to 200°C / 400°F. Pierce each eggplant several times with a fork and set on the tray.
2. Roast for approximately 30–40 minutes, turning once. The whole eggplant must feel collapsed and soft under a fork; continue if the center resists.
3. Cool until safe to handle. Peel away skin and flatten the flesh gently with a fork, leaving the stem attached. Do not handle hot eggplant by the stem alone.
4. Beat eggs, salt, and optional pepper. Coat one flattened eggplant with half the egg mixture.
5. Warm 15ml oil over medium heat. Use the spatula to lower the coated eggplant into the pan, adding its allocated egg. Cook about 3–4 minutes per side, turning gently. Aim for a golden surface and completely set egg; the egg portion should reach 71°C / 160°F.
6. Repeat with remaining oil, eggplant, and egg. Serve one omelet per portion.

**Optional explainer:** Let the underside set before turning. A broad spatula supports the soft vegetable better than pulling its stem.

**Test questions:** Does this eggplant size cook within the window? Is the egg coating sufficient, and does it release cleanly? A large globe eggplant is a separate variant needing testing, not an automatic one-for-one swap.

**Reference and changes:** Lalaine Manalo's [Tortang Talong, Kawaling Pinoy](https://www.kawalingpinoy.com/tortang-talong/) describes the dish and an oven option. This draft chooses the oven rather than open-flame cooking, specifies batch oil, and adds measurable egg doneness. Record the changed texture and absence of charred flavor.

## R03 — Ginisang Sayote with Chicken

**Status:** Untested. **Serves:** 2. **Equipment:** board, knife/peeler, wide lidded pan, spoon, food thermometer; food-safe gloves for sayote preparation. **Contains:** oyster sauce, typically mollusc and possibly soy/wheat; check its label.

| Ingredient | Test quantity | Household description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boneless, skinless chicken thigh | 250g | Cut into roughly 2cm pieces |
| Sayote, peeled and cored | 400g | About 2 small chayotes, thinly sliced; prepared weight controls |
| Onion, prepared | 100g | About 1 small onion, chopped |
| Garlic, prepared | 6g | About 2 cloves, chopped |
| Tomato, prepared | 150g | About 2 small tomatoes, chopped |
| Neutral oil | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Water | 180ml | ¾ test cup |
| Oyster sauce | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | Optional |

1. Wear gloves to peel sayote if its sap irritates your skin. Remove the seed/core and slice about 5mm thick. Prepare remaining vegetables before the chicken.
2. Heat oil over medium heat. Stir onion for 4 minutes, then garlic for 30 seconds.
3. Add chicken and stir for about 4 minutes. Add tomatoes and cook another 3 minutes, until they soften.
4. Stir in water and oyster sauce. Cover and maintain gentle bubbles for approximately 8 minutes. Check chicken reaches 74°C / 165°F; continue cooking as needed.
5. Add sayote. Cover for about 5 minutes, then check every 2 minutes until a fork enters with a little resistance. Keep some broth; add a spoonful of water if needed.
6. Add optional pepper. Taste before adding any extra salt because oyster sauce is already seasoned. Divide into two portions.

**Optional explainer:** Staged cooking means adding ingredients at different times so the chicken cooks through while the vegetable keeps some bite.

**Test questions:** Is 180ml water appropriate for the test pan? Record sayote tenderness and total time. A mushroom-based sauce is a separate substitution trial; do not mark it allergen-free without checking the product.

**Reference and changes:** Lalaine Manalo's [Ginisang Sayote with Chicken, Kawaling Pinoy](https://www.kawalingpinoy.com/ginisang-sayote-with-chicken/) supplies the dish reference. This draft specifies prepared vegetable mass, a smaller portion, revised water amount, and a thermometer checkpoint. Source times and nutrition do not transfer.

## R04 — Tomato and Garlic Pasta

**Status:** Untested. **Serves:** 2. **Equipment:** saucepan, frying pan, colander, heatproof cup, spoon. **Contains:** usually wheat; pasta may contain egg.

| Ingredient | Test quantity | Household description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry short pasta | 200g | Weigh; cup quantity varies with shape |
| Canned chopped tomatoes with juice | 400g | 1 can labelled 400g |
| Garlic, prepared | 6g | About 2 cloves, chopped |
| Olive oil | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Tomato paste/concentrate | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Water for boiling pasta | 2 litres | Use the pot's marked capacity |
| Fine salt for pasta water | 1 tsp | Separate from sauce seasoning |
| Fine salt for sauce | Up to ¼ tsp | Add gradually to taste |
| Fresh basil leaves | About 5 leaves | Optional |
| Reserved pasta water | Up to 60ml | Up to 4 tbsp, taken from the cooking pot |

1. Bring the 2 litres water to a boil. Add pasta-water salt and pasta; stir. Use the packet's cooking time as the starting timer.
2. Meanwhile warm oil over medium-low heat. Cook garlic for about 30 seconds, keeping it pale. Stir in tomato paste for another 30 seconds.
3. Add tomatoes. Simmer uncovered for about 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce is less watery.
4. Taste a pasta piece near the packet time; it should be tender with slight resistance. Before draining, use the heatproof cup to reserve water carefully. Drain without splashing.
5. Toss pasta into the sauce for 1 minute. Add reserved water by the tablespoon until sauce coats it. Add optional torn basil; season sauce gradually with the remaining salt allowance.

**Optional explainer:** Starchy cooking water helps loosen sauce while it clings to pasta. It is not a second pantry ingredient or a second consumption of water.

**Test questions:** Record pasta brand/shape and drained yield. Is the sauce sufficient without added sugar or stock? Gluten-free pasta needs its own packet timing and texture trial.

**Reference and changes:** Good Food's [Tomato sauce for pasta](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/tomato-basil-sauce) supplies a sauce reference. This draft adds a complete pasta workflow, omits stock powder and sugar, changes garlic/timing, and uses reserved cooking water. The source method mentions sugar absent from its retrieved ingredient list; this draft does not inherit that inconsistency.

## R05 — Chickpea, Tomato and Spinach Curry

**Status:** Untested. **Serves:** 2 generous portions. **Equipment:** sieve, deep pan, knife, board, spoon. **Allergens:** check curry powder and coconut milk labels; assess ingredients and cross-contact individually.

| Ingredient | Test quantity | Household description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed | 480g drained | Usually 2 cans labelled 400g; check drained weights |
| Canned chopped tomatoes with juice | 400g | 1 can labelled 400g |
| Coconut milk, stirred | 200ml | ¾ test cup + 1 tbsp + 1 tsp |
| Fresh spinach | 100g | Weigh; packed cup volume varies |
| Onion, prepared | 100g | About 1 small onion, chopped |
| Garlic, prepared | 6g | About 2 cloves, chopped |
| Fresh ginger, prepared | 10g | Finely chopped; weigh |
| Neutral oil | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Mild curry powder | 2 tsp | Record brand and ingredients |
| Fine salt | Up to ¼ tsp | Add gradually to taste |
| Water | Up to 60ml | Up to 4 tbsp, only if needed |

1. Drain chickpeas and prepare vegetables. Set ingredients near the hob before heating the pan.
2. Warm oil over medium heat. Stir onion for about 5 minutes, until softened. Add garlic and ginger for 1 minute.
3. Stir curry powder through for 30 seconds, then immediately add tomatoes so spices do not scorch.
4. Add chickpeas and coconut milk. Simmer gently, uncovered, for about 12–15 minutes, stirring. Add measured water only if the mixture catches before heating through.
5. Fold in spinach for 2–3 minutes until wilted throughout. Taste and add salt gradually. Divide into two bowls; rice is separate.

**Optional explainer:** Spices can turn bitter when burned. Have the tomatoes open before briefly heating the powder.

**Test questions:** Are portions too large? Does the named curry blend taste balanced? Coconut milk replaces the reference's creamed coconut, and curry powder replaces its blended spice paste; neither is a like-for-like conversion. A variation without coconut requires a separate trial.

**Reference and changes:** Good Food's [Chickpea curry, credited to reader Jon Gregg](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/kadala-curry), informs the chickpea/tomato/spinach combination. This draft removes the food processor, uses a simpler seasoning system, and changes coconut form and serving yield. It is not presented as a traditional kadala curry.

## R06 — Shakshuka

**Status:** Untested. **Serves:** 2 light portions. **Equipment:** 20–22cm frying pan with lid, spoon, bowl. **Contains:** egg.

| Ingredient | Test quantity | Household description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large eggs | 2 | 1 per portion |
| Canned chopped tomatoes with juice | 400g | 1 can labelled 400g |
| Onion, prepared | 100g | About 1 small onion, chopped |
| Olive oil | 15ml | 1 tbsp |
| Fine salt | Up to ¼ tsp | Add gradually to taste |
| Garlic, prepared | 6g | About 2 cloves, optional |
| Ground paprika | 1 tsp | Optional |
| Ground cumin | ½ tsp | Optional |
| Water | Up to 30ml | Up to 2 tbsp, if sauce dries |

1. Warm oil over medium heat. Stir onion for about 5 minutes until softened. If using garlic, add it for 30 seconds.
2. Add optional spices, immediately followed by tomatoes. Simmer for about 10–12 minutes. Stir until sauce is thick enough to hold two shallow hollows; season gradually with salt.
3. Crack each egg into a small bowl, then slide into a hollow. Cover and lower heat so sauce bubbles gently.
4. Begin checking after 7 minutes; continue until whites and yolks are firm. Add measured water around the edge if the sauce starts catching. A timer is a reminder to check, not proof of doneness.
5. Spoon one egg and half the sauce into each bowl. Bread is a separate accompaniment.

**Optional explainer:** Gentle heat and a lid help set the tops while avoiding a burned sauce base.

**Test questions:** Is one egg a sufficient portion? Record pan diameter, lid fit, and time until firm yolks. This draft preserves the sample's 2 eggs / 400g tomatoes / 100g onion foundation, but it is a separate content record. Do not overwrite or claim identical pantry scores to the existing demo: optional ingredients can affect its weighted score.

**Reference and changes:** Lulu Grimes's [Shakshuka, Good Food](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/shakshuka) supplies a covered-pan technique reference. This draft changes the egg-to-tomato ratio, makes aromatics/spices optional, omits sugar/chilli/coriander, and uses fully firm eggs under the linked official guidance.

## Kitchen testing and release gate

Suggested test order: R04, R06, R03, R01, R02, R05. This is an editorial recommendation to move from familiar pot/pan work toward more handling and seasoning decisions.

For each recipe:

1. **Editorial review:** Confirm ingredient identity, complete quantities, allergens, cultural naming, equipment, and source/change notes. Check that every required ingredient is used and every used ingredient is listed.
2. **Baseline cook:** Cook the two-serving draft exactly once without substitutions. Record ingredient brands, actual prepared weights, pan dimensions, hob/oven settings, measured temperatures where applicable, active/elapsed time, final yield, and deviations. Photograph the result using original or permitted assets.
3. **Beginner cook:** Have a beginner follow the revised card without verbal coaching. Log confusing terms, missing preparation, timing surprises, and whether sensory cues helped. Obtain permission before collecting any tester media or identifying details.
4. **Revision and repeat:** Fix failures and repeat the relevant test. Proposed promotion requirement: one successful baseline and one successful beginner cook of the same final version. This count is a project quality proposal, not proof of universal reliability.
5. **Variant checks:** Test a half/double batch, each offered substitution, and household-measure display independently before enabling those variants. Record dry versus drained versus prepared mass consistently.
6. **App checks after approval:** Map real canonical ingredient IDs, mark required/optional/staple roles, add technique links, and verify scaling, offline access, timer reminders, and consumption preview. Keep recipe version fixed during an active session.

Automated moderation approval is a publication check, not kitchen-test approval. Do not mark these drafts “Lu2 tested” or load them into a public curated collection before the test records support that label.
